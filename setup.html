Setup guide

Getting WickFlow Pro running

Ten minutes, most of it waiting for MetaTrader to restart. Two sections matter more than the rest: the one about WebRequest, which is what stops nine out of ten first runs, and the one about the symbol list, which is the only input with a syntax of its own.

Install

Your purchase email carries two things: the file WickFlowPro.ex5 and a licence key that looks like WSF-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX. Keep the email; the key is not shown anywhere else.

  1. Put the file where MetaTrader looks

    In MetaTrader: File → Open Data Folder → MQL5 → Experts. Drop WickFlowPro.ex5 there. Back in the terminal, right-click the Navigator and choose Refresh, or restart MetaTrader.

  2. Allow the licence address

    Tools → Options → Expert Advisors. Tick Allow WebRequest for listed URL and add https://api.wisfli.com — exactly that, no trailing slash. On a VPS this has to be done in the VPS terminal, which is a different terminal from the one on your desk. That is the step people forget.

  3. Attach it to any chart

    Any symbol, any timeframe. WickFlow Pro does not trade the chart it sits on: it trades the symbols in its list, on the timeframes in its list. The chart is only where it lives.

  4. Paste the key and write the symbol list

    Both are in the inputs. Enable algorithmic trading with the toolbar button, then read the Journal before you walk away.

Check the Journal before you walk away

On a good start you get three things: a line with the inputs it actually received, one line per symbol showing the timeframes it built, and a line per pair with its magic number. Then the licence line, which should say License active.

If you see Allow WebRequest for https://api.wisfli.com, go back to step two. If a symbol is missing from the list, the EA says so by name — usually a broker suffix.

Writing the symbol list

The only input with a syntax. Two shapes are accepted and the EA prints what it understood, so you can check it rather than guess.

A plain list

Symbols separated by commas. Every one of them uses the timeframes from the Default timeframes input.

Symbols     XAUUSD,EURUSD,USDJPY Timeframes  H1,H4

That builds six pairs: each symbol on H1 and on H4.

One entry per symbol

Put a colon after the symbol and list its own timeframes. Separate the entries with a pipe or a semicolon.

Symbols     XAUUSD:H1,H2|USDJPY:H4

That builds three pairs. A symbol written without a colon still falls back to the default list, so the two shapes can be mixed.

If your broker adds a suffix

Many brokers name the symbol XAUUSD.m or EURUSD_raw. Write the plain names in the list and put the suffix in the Broker suffix input. The EA tries the plain name first and then the name with the suffix.

Magic numbers, and why they matter

Each timeframe gets a slot, and each pair's magic is the base plus that slot. With a base of 771001 and the timeframes H1 and H2, the magics are 771001 and 771002. The EA prints them at startup.

You need them twice: to tell two copies of the EA apart on the same account, and because the per-symbol report groups its results by them. Write them down from the Journal rather than working them out — the slot order depends on the list.

How many is too many

Every pair means its own indicator handles and its own study. Past roughly sixty pairs the EA warns you that the tester will crawl. A handful you have actually tested beats a long list you have not.

What the inputs do

Grouped the way they appear in the dialog. The defaults are the ones running on the Armada account; once you change them, the published figures no longer describe your setup.

Symbols and general
Licence keyYour WSF- key. Leave it empty in the Strategy Tester.
SymbolsThe list. See the section above.
Broker suffixAppended when the plain name is not found, e.g. .m.
Default timeframesUsed by any symbol written without its own.
Allowed directionBoth, longs only or shorts only. Useful once the report tells you one side is doing all the damage.
Reverse every signalThe control test. If the EA makes money reversed, the logic was systematically wrong rather than neutral. Leave it off in normal use.
Magic numberThe base. Each timeframe slot adds one.
Max positions per symbol / in total1 and 3 by default. The total cap is your protection against several correlated symbols firing at once.
CooldownBars to wait after an entry before that pair may enter again.
Strategy
Which strategyWickFlow, price action, either of them, range, or regime. One at a time, on purpose: a blend hides which part is working.
WickFlow groupHow small the body has to be, how big the wicks, whether both wicks must qualify, and how far back the MFI extreme may be. The candle is the trigger; the MFI extreme and the SAR are the context.
Price action groupWhich of the four patterns are live, whether they must appear at a swing level, how deep a swing has to be, and whether to trade with the structure or against it.
Range groupBars that define the range, minimum and maximum width in ATR, how close to the edge counts as the entry zone, the target as a percentage of the width, and the bar limit that acts as the expiry.
Regime groupMinimum state probability to act, the dead zone that decides whether a state counts as directional, whether to trade only on a state change, and whether to close when the state stops favouring the trade.
Confirmations — each one optional, each one mandatory when on
Bollinger bandsThe signal candle must reach the band it is reverting from, within a tolerance given as a percentage of the band width.
VolumeThe signal candle needs a volume spike against the average of the previous bars, and optionally the highest of them.
ADX / DMIA ceiling so a reversal is not taken against a very strong trend, an optional floor, and a requirement that the opposing DMI line is already falling.
IchimokuOff, discount (buy only below the Kijun), or trend (buy only with Tenkan above Kijun). Tenkan and Kijun only — no cloud, so there is no ambiguity about the forward shift.
Parabolic SARStep and maximum, how recent the flip may be, and whether to close the position when the SAR flips against it.
Stops, targets and management
Stop loss modeAn ATR multiple, or beyond the signal candle's wick with an ATR buffer. The second is the default.
Take profit modeNone, an ATR multiple, a multiple of the stop distance, or the next opposite swing. A structural target closer than the stop falls back to the risk multiple.
Break-evenTrigger in R (the initial stop distance) or in ATR, and how much to lock beyond entry.
TrailingOff, an ATR distance, or a second Parabolic SAR with its own step. The minimum improvement before the stop is moved is there to stop it nagging the broker.
Partial closeTake a percentage off at a trigger in R or ATR. Off by default.
The study
Bars scanned / horizonHow much history each study walks, and how many bars each signal is followed for. A longer horizon measures a slower strategy; it also means fewer signals whose horizon has fully closed.
Refit every N barsHow often the study runs again. Lower is more responsive and slower to test.
Minimum signalsBelow this the study keeps the previous result instead of trusting a thin sample.
Minimum expectancyIn R. A side whose out-of-sample expectancy falls below this stops trading until a later study clears it.
Validation %The share of the newest signals held back from choosing the settings. Half by default. Lowering it makes the chosen settings look better and mean less.
Monte Carlo runs / percentile / confidenceHow many resamples, which percentile of them the EA trusts instead of the average, and what share of them must stay above zero.
Half-lifeBars after which an old signal counts half. Keeps the sample large while letting the recent past drive the answer.
Blend with the inputs0 uses your stop and target, 1 uses the learned ones, 0.5 splits the difference. It exists so one study cannot swing the EA on its own.
Brakes and filters
Drawdown brakeNo new trades past this distance from the equity peak. It releases when the account recovers, or after a set number of hours — without that release it would be a trap, since a stopped EA can never earn the equity back.
Losing-streak pauseAfter N losing trades in a row, pause for N bars.
Daily loss stopPercentage of the day's opening equity. Off by default.
SpreadA ceiling in points, or as a percentage of ATR — the second travels between instruments, the first does not.
Session and FridayAn optional hour window in server time, and an hour after which no new trades are opened on Friday.
Risk per tradeA fixed lot, or a percentage of balance risked to the stop. There is no multiplier after a loss anywhere in the code.

Backtesting

The free build runs only here. It is the same logic as the paid one, so what you measure is what you would run.

Settings that matter

Modelling: Every tick based on real ticks. Anything faster will flatter a system that reads closed bars on several timeframes at once.

Spread: your broker's, or realistic. Never a fixed low value. On the faster timeframes the spread is a large part of the result, and a test at one point of spread is a test of nothing.

Pick one chart symbol and let the input list do the rest. The tester will download history for every symbol in the list, which is why the first run is slow.

The report is written to a text file as well as the Journal: File → Open Data Folder, go up one level to Common\Files. The Journal is muted during optimisation; the file is not.

What you cannot see in a test

Requotes, slippage on a fast fill, and the way a spread widens around news. The tester is optimistic about all three.

The regime model is refitted forward in time during a tester run, which is the honest way to measure it. Inside a single study, older bars are scored with a model fitted later — that is why the tester run is the verdict and the study's own numbers are only a guide.

Two runs worth doing

One with the study off, so you see the raw strategy. One with the signals reversed, which tells you whether a flat result was neutral or systematically wrong. Both take the same time as a normal run and both are more informative than another parameter sweep.

When something goes wrong

Almost everything the EA refuses to do, it explains in the Journal first.

Startup and runtime messages
symbol '…' is not available, skippedThe name does not exist at your broker. Usually a suffix; fill the suffix input.
'…' is not a timeframeUse names like M15, M30, H1, H2, H4, D1.
no timeframes of its own, it takes the defaultNot an error. That symbol had no colon.
nothing usable in '…', falling back to …The whole list failed to resolve, so the EA is trading the chart symbol only. Fix the list.
that is a lot of pairs, the tester will be slowOver sixty pairs. Not an error, a warning about your evening.
study skipped, only N signalsNot enough history yet, or the filters are too tight. The previous result is kept.
signal skipped, the study says this side has no edgeWorking as intended. That side is off until a later study clears it.
signal skipped, adverse regimeThe regime filter blocked it. The state and its probability are in the line.
stop too close to price, trade skippedThe broker's minimum stop distance is wider than the stop the strategy wanted. Widen the ATR multiple or the buffer.
lot size came out as 0The risk percentage produced less than the minimum lot, or free margin is too low.
order failed. retcode=…The broker refused it. The description is on the same line; the usual causes are filling mode and market closed.
drawdown brake ONNo new trades until the account recovers or the release time passes. Open positions keep being managed.
