Multi-symbol · MetaTrader 5 · one chart

Five strategies.
One that keeps checking itself.

WickFlow Pro trades a list of symbols and timeframes from a single chart. You pick which strategy runs — an indecision-candle reversal, a price-action engine, a range system, or a regime model that fits itself to the market. Each symbol and timeframe gets its own magic number, so the results come back separated.

What makes it different is what it does between trades. Every few bars it replays its own recent history, finds every bar where it would have fired, measures what actually happened next, and switches off the sides and the regimes that have stopped paying.

Closed bars only

Every rule is read on the bar that has just finished. Nothing is evaluated mid-bar, so what the tester shows is what runs live.

Fixed size, always

Either a fixed lot or a fixed percentage of balance risked per trade. There is no grid, no averaging down and no lot multiplier after a loss — anywhere in the code.

It can switch itself off

If the study cannot find a positive expectancy on the recent history, that side simply stops trading until the next study says otherwise.

The five engines

One input chooses which one runs. They share the same filters, the same position management and the same study, so you can compare them on the same symbol without changing anything else.

WickFlow — the original

An indecision candle (small body, wicks larger than the body), a Money Flow Index extreme a few bars earlier that has since turned, and the Parabolic SAR already on the right side. Bollinger touch, volume spike, ADX ceiling and an Ichimoku filter are each optional and each a hard requirement when switched on.

Price action — no indicators

Swing structure and four patterns: engulfing bar, pin bar with a liquidity sweep of the recent extreme, inside-bar breakout, and break of a swing followed by a retest. You choose whether it trades with the structure, against it at levels, or either.

Range — the condor bet, without options

It finds a range the market is respecting, fades each edge back towards the middle with the stop outside the range, and closes on a bar limit that plays the part of an expiry. Set it the other way and it takes the breakout instead, or let it decide by drift and ADX.

Regime — a model, not a pattern

A Gaussian hidden Markov model is fitted to the last few hundred bars with Baum-Welch, on two features: the bar's return and its range, both normalised so the same settings work on gold and on a currency pair. States are relabelled by mean return, so state 0 is always the most bearish. The EA takes the side the current state leans to.

Anything that cannot be computed blocks the entry

If an indicator buffer is not ready, or there is not enough history for the swing scan, the condition is not active and the trade does not happen. The EA never assumes a value in order to trade.

Before the order

Spread ceiling in points or as a percentage of ATR, a cap per symbol and in total, a cooldown in bars since the last entry, an optional session window, a Friday cutoff and a daily loss stop.

While the trade is open

Stop from the ATR or from beyond the signal candle's wick, target as an ATR multiple, a risk multiple or the next opposite swing. Break-even, trailing by ATR or by a second Parabolic SAR, a partial close, and an exit when the SAR flips or the regime turns.

When things go wrong

A drawdown brake stops new trades past a set distance from the equity peak and releases itself after a set number of hours, so it cannot lock the account out forever. A run of losing trades pauses the EA for a number of bars.

The part that does the arguing

Most Expert Advisors are a fixed set of rules that keep firing whether or not they still work. WickFlow Pro carries a study that re-runs on its own history every few bars and is allowed to veto the strategy.

It measures signals, not its own trades

Waiting for the EA's closed trades would mean learning from a handful of samples. Instead the study walks back through the recent bars, asks the exact same function that decides live entries what it would have said on each one, and then measures what price actually did over the following bars. That yields hundreds of samples instead of a dozen, and because it is the same function, what gets measured is what gets traded.

It chooses on one half and validates on the other

The stop, target and trailing distances are picked by a small grid search on the older part of the sample. They are then measured on the newest part, which took no role in choosing them. This is the whole point: the best of twenty-seven combinations is high by luck almost every time, and the only way to find out is to test it on data that did not help pick it.

It bootstraps what is left

The validated results are resampled a few hundred times. The EA does not trust the average, it trusts a low percentile of those resamples, and it also needs a minimum share of them to have stayed above zero. Older signals count for less, on a half-life you set, so a strategy that worked two years ago and not since does not keep voting.

And then it switches things off

Longs and shorts are judged separately. If one side cannot clear the minimum expectancy with enough confidence, that side stops trading until a later study says otherwise — the other side carries on. In regime mode the same is done state by state: each hidden state gets its own out-of-sample expectancy, and the ones that do not pay are switched off individually.

The control test is built in

One input reverses every signal the EA produces. If a strategy makes money reversed, it was not neutral — it was systematically wrong, which is worth knowing before you conclude anything from a flat equity curve.

And it tells you what to cut

When the EA is removed — including at the end of a backtest — it prints three tables: results per symbol and timeframe with a first-cut verdict, how the trades ended (initial stop, break-even, target, closed by the EA), and results grouped by the regime at entry. The same tables are written to a text file in the shared folder, because the tester Journal is easy to miss.

What WickFlow Pro is not

It is not a signal service and not a managed account: you run it on your own terminal and you keep control of the settings. It does not promise a return.

The study is a measurement, not a guarantee. It can only describe the recent past of the symbol it is watching, and it re-measures precisely because that past stops applying. A strategy it has approved can still lose, and the drawdown brake exists because that is expected rather than hypothetical.

Running several symbols at once means several positions can be open at the same time, and correlated instruments will move together. The total cap is the control for that, and it is set to three by default for a reason.

How the licence works

WickFlow Pro is licensed software. After payment you receive the compiled .ex5 by email together with a key, and the EA checks that key when it starts and while it runs.

Your key

The key looks like WSF-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX. You paste it into the EA inputs. One key works on up to three MetaTrader accounts, so your demo, your live account and a backup terminal are all covered.

The check is made against api.wisfli.com over HTTPS. The EA sends the key and the account number, nothing else — no trade data, no credentials, no personal information.

Allow the address in MetaTrader

MetaTrader blocks outbound requests unless you list the address. Open Tools → Options → Expert Advisors, tick Allow WebRequest for listed URL and add https://api.wisfli.com. This is the single most common reason a first run fails.

If you cancel

You keep access until the end of the period you already paid for. After that the EA stops opening new positions — and only that. Existing positions are never closed for you: stops, targets, break-even, trailing, the partial close and the regime exit all keep working, so nothing is left unmanaged.

Questions

If yours is not here, write to support@wisfli.com.

Can I backtest it before subscribing?

Yes, and you should. The backtest build is free and needs no key. It contains the same logic as the paid version but only runs inside the Strategy Tester: on a live or demo chart it prints a line in the Journal and does nothing. Test it on your own broker's data, because the spread and commission are a large part of the result.

How do I write the symbol list?

Two shapes. A plain list — XAUUSD,EURUSD — gives every symbol the timeframes from the default input. Or one entry per symbol with its own — XAUUSD:H1,H2|USDJPY:H4 — separated by a pipe or a semicolon. If your broker adds a suffix like .m, put it in the suffix input and write the plain names.

Which strategy should I run?

Run one at a time and compare the reports. They are deliberately not blended, because a blend hides which part is working. The setup guide suggests a starting order, but the honest answer is that it depends on your symbols and your broker, and the per-symbol table exists so you can find out rather than guess.

What does the study actually turn off?

Longs and shorts independently, and in regime mode each hidden state independently. A side stops trading when its out-of-sample expectancy on recent signals falls below the minimum you set, or when too few of the bootstrap resamples stay above zero. It turns back on by itself when a later study clears it.

Does the study look into the future?

The signals it measures only use data up to the bar they fire on, and only signals whose horizon has fully closed are counted. The one honest caveat is in regime mode: inside a single study, older bars are scored with a model fitted later. That is why the Strategy Tester run is the verdict — there the model is refitted forward in time, the way it runs live.

The EA says it cannot reach the licence server.

Nine times out of ten the address is not allowed in MetaTrader. Add https://api.wisfli.com under Tools → Options → Expert Advisors → Allow WebRequest for listed URL, exactly like that, with no trailing slash. If it still fails, a firewall or the VPS provider may be blocking outbound HTTPS.

What happens to my trades if the subscription ends?

New entries stop. Everything else continues: stop loss, take profit, break-even, trailing, the partial close, the SAR flip exit and the regime exit are all still applied, so no position is abandoned. Nothing is force-closed by the licence.

Can I use it on more than one account?

Yes, up to three MetaTrader accounts with the same key — typically a demo, a live account and a spare terminal. Sharing the file or the key outside your own accounts is not permitted and is the one thing that gets a licence revoked.

Do demo accounts need a licence?

Yes. The subscription covers the software, not the account type, so a demo chart validates the key exactly as a live one does. Only the Strategy Tester runs without a key — that is what the free backtest build is for.

How many symbols can it handle?

There is no hard limit, but every symbol and timeframe pair means its own indicator handles and its own study. Past roughly sixty pairs the EA warns you in the Journal that the tester will be slow. In practice a handful of pairs you have actually tested beats a long list you have not.

Can I change the settings?

Every parameter is exposed as an input: strategy, thresholds, stops, filters, sizing, study window, brakes. The defaults are what runs on the Armada account, so if you change them the published figures no longer describe your setup.
